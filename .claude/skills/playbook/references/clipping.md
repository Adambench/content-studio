# Clipping

A clip is a moment cut from something long (a stream, a podcast, an interview, a talk) that works on its own as a
short. Picking the moment is the decision that matters most: a perfect edit can't save a moment that needs context,
and a strong moment survives a plain edit.

## The campaign's rules come first

On a campaign, the brief decides before this playbook does (after the halal rules, which come before everything):

- **Read the whole rule checklist before picking anything.** Allowed source material, required mentions, tags,
  hashtags or text, credits, length limits, allowed platforms, language, banned edit styles, watermark rules.
- Use **only the source material the campaign allows**.
- Every clip's captions include the **required mentions and hashtags** for each platform it goes on.
- Length limits from the brief replace the defaults below.
- A rule you can't check from the transcript (the speaker must be on screen, a logo must show) is listed for the
  user to tick on review.
- **Past rejection reasons are rules too.** If the user rejected clips for something, don't pick that again.

## What a good moment is

Moments that clip well, from strongest to weakest on average:

| Kind | What counts |
|---|---|
| **Story with a turn** | a problem, a decision, what happened; the turn is inside the clip |
| **Strong claim** | a clear position, ideally against the usual advice, with the reason given |
| **Number or result** | a price, a before and after, a time, a count, said plainly |
| **Mistake** | what went wrong and what they learned |
| **Reaction or reveal** | a big laugh, a shock, an argument, a result revealed: the reaction is the payoff |
| **How-to** | one move or a few steps the viewer can apply |
| **Answer** | a question the audience really asks, answered bluntly |

On streams, add: a challenge's result, a heated exchange, a funny bit that lands without the chat, a guest's
surprising line. A moment is only a candidate if it fits the page's niche and the campaign's brief.

The app's scan finds candidate windows mechanically (loudness peaks, speech pace, chat spikes, the brief's keywords).
Those are hints where something happened, not verdicts: read the words.

## Standalone, or it isn't a clip

Each moment must:

- **Open cold.** The first line makes sense to someone who saw nothing before. Never start on "so", "and", "but",
  "like I said", "this", "that's why", or a "he", "she" or "it" nobody introduced.
- **Hold one idea**, complete. No "and also".
- **End clean**, on a finished thought: not mid-list, not on "and then", not on a question someone else answers
  after the cut.
- **Need nothing outside it.** No "as I said earlier", "this guest", "the next part", "in this video", "look at
  this slide", "chat, look".
- **Run 20 to 60 seconds** (unless the brief says otherwise).

Score each point 0 to 2 and keep 7 or more out of 10:

| Point | 2 when | 1 when | 0 when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opens cold | the first line works with no context | it needs one obvious word of context | it starts mid-thought |
| One idea | one point, start to finish | a small side step | two ideas |
| Ends clean | stops on a full, final sentence | ends on a sentence that trails | stops mid-thought |
| No outside reference | none | one minor reference a hook card can cover | it depends on something earlier |
| Length | 20–60 s | 15–20 s or 60–75 s | under 15 s or over 75 s |

## In and out points

- **Cut on sentence boundaries.** In: the first word of the line that works cold. Out: the end of the payoff line,
  plus a beat (about 0.3–0.5 s). If the reaction is the payoff (a laugh, a stunned pause), keep the reaction and cut
  right after it.
- **Start late.** Skip the setup if the turn explains itself. A story clipped from its middle, at the moment it
  goes wrong, beats the same story from "so last year…".
- **End early.** Stop as soon as the idea lands. The recap, the "anyway", the change of subject all go.
- Keep it one continuous stretch where you can. Cutting out a tangent inside is fine only when the meaning stays
  exactly the same.
- Transcript times are approximate: the app cuts with margins and trims to the word afterwards. Give times as
  precisely as the transcript allows.
- Moments from one source don't overlap, and spread across it: the best clips aren't always in the loud part.

## The hook

Choosing where the clip starts is writing its hook (`hooks.md`). Prefer a start whose first line is itself a strong
hook. When the best moment starts weakly, keep it and write a **hook card**: 6 words or fewer, one accent word, on
screen for the first ~2.5 s, saying what the clip is about to deliver.

The hook card and the title describe the moment truthfully. Never claim more than the clip shows, and never put a
quote in someone's mouth.

## Never misrepresent the source or the speaker

- No cut that changes what someone meant: no splicing sentences into a claim they didn't make, no dropping the "not"
  or the "but" that reverses it.
- No out-of-context framing that makes a joke look serious, or a question look like an answer.
- Captions show what was said. Fix misheard words; don't improve them.
- Credit the source the way the campaign asks.

## Make it the page's own

Platforms and campaigns flag duplicates and low-effort reposts (a raw snippet, the same cut every clipper posts).
Each clip should be transformed: burned-in word-timed captions, a hook card, tightened pauses, punch-ins, reframing
on the speaker, sparing sound effects, the page's look. Across clips from the same source, and across pages, vary
the opening frame, the crop, the hook card and the caption style. The same moment on two pages gets a different
hook, caption and crop for each.

## Halal checks on every moment

- **Music in the background:** prefer stretches with none. The app isolates the voice and rejects clips it can't
  clean, but a moment where a song is the point (someone singing, a music break, a reaction to a track) is skipped
  outright.
- **What's said and what's shown:** drop moments that promote or show anything in `halal.md` (gambling on stream,
  drinking, immodest dress, a betting sponsor read). Flag the gray areas with the reason.

## How many

3 to 8 moments per source, only those that score 7 or more. Fewer good ones beat more weak ones: a source full of
context ("as we saw", "this slide") gives few clips, and that's fine. Mix the kinds: not five reactions from one
stream.

## What to give for each moment

- in and out points (seconds), and the length
- the spoken first line, word for word
- a hook card (6 words or fewer), or none if the first line carries it
- a title (`packaging.md`)
- why it works, in one line: the kind, the turn or payoff, who it's for
- the standalone score (out of 10)
- captions for each platform the campaign allows, with the required mentions and hashtags
- halal flags (or none), and any campaign rule the user must check by eye

## How campaigns pay (Content Rewards)

- Pay is per 1,000 verified views on approved submissions, usually with a minimum view count before anything is
  paid and a cap per clip. Typical rates are a few tenths of a dollar to a few dollars per 1,000.
- Budgets are finite and first come, first served: a fresh, well-funded campaign rewards clipping early; a campaign
  near the end of its budget may not pay for views still being counted.
- Views count from when the post is submitted and trackable, only on accounts linked to Whop. Submit as soon as a
  clip is live. The app's stats readings (24 h, 3 days, a week) are the evidence if the counts disagree.
- Clips get rejected for missed rules (a missing tag or credit, wrong source, banned style, too short), for
  duplicates, and for submitting after the budget ran out.
- Volume matters because rates are low: many good clips over time, not one perfect one.
