# Platforms

What TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts reward now. A workspace is one page: its TikTok, Instagram and
YouTube accounts post the same video, each with its own caption. Treat the numbers here as directional: platforms
change, and the page's own analytics win over any figure below.

## All three

- **Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920**, full frame. Captions burned in.
- **Each post is judged on its own.** A new post goes to a small test group (mostly people who don't follow the
  page), then wider if they watch, rewatch, share and save. Follower count barely matters for one post, which is why
  a small page can break out.
- **The first seconds and completion are the gate.** Then rewatches and shares. Likes matter least.
- **Search reads everything:** spoken words, on-screen text, caption, title. Say the topic word out loud, show it,
  write it.
- **Original audio.** Trending sounds are out (`halal.md`), and original audio tends to do better for smaller
  accounts anyway.
- **One idea per video.** A second idea is a second post.
- **Volume, then double down.** Nobody can pick winners up front: post consistently, read the results, make more of
  what worked.

## Safe zones (1080×1920)

Keep text, faces and anything important out of the platforms' buttons and captions:

| | Top | Bottom | Right | Left |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | ~130 px | ~440–480 px | ~120 px | ~60 px |
| Reels | ~130 px | ~600 px | ~150 px | ~60 px |
| Shorts | ~130 px | ~30 % of the height | ~120 px | ~60 px |

The middle of the frame is safe everywhere. Keep the speaker's eyes in the upper third.

## TikTok

- Ranks on what each viewer is interested in, not who they follow. Completion and rewatches first, then shares,
  saves and comments that start replies.
- The first ~100–150 characters of the caption feed search and tell TikTok who to test the video on.
- Short clips (about 21–34 s) finish best; longer ones need a re-hook to hold.
- Repetitive, low-effort posting is penalised. Don't post near-identical clips.
- Old posts can come back months later when they get shared or searched: don't delete underperformers.
- In the app: posts go to the TikTok inbox until the app passes TikTok's audit (the user taps Post in TikTok, and
  the caption is pasted from Copy caption). "Auto add music" is always off.

## Instagram Reels

- **Sends (DM shares) count most** for reaching people who don't follow the page, then watch time, then likes.
- **Originality:** accounts that mostly post unoriginal content stop being recommended to non-followers. A repost
  with a border, a watermark, a speed change or a credit line isn't original; your own cuts, captions, hook text and
  commentary are. It's judged over the last 30 days of posts.
- **No other platform's watermark** (TikTok, CapCut) anywhere in the frame: it cuts reach silently. The page's own
  logo is fine.
- The caption is Instagram's main search signal; hashtags only label.
- Cover: cropped to the centre in the profile grid.

## YouTube Shorts

- **Viewed vs swiped away** is the first gate (creators report 70–90 % for breakouts, under 60 % rarely spreads).
  Then the share of the video watched and loops: a rewatch counts, so watched can go over 100 %.
- Shares weigh heavily; likes and comments barely move it.
- Shorts can be up to 3 minutes, but length follows retention.
- Title and the first lines of the description are read for search, and Shorts keep getting found in search for
  months: evergreen topics pay off here.
- `#Shorts` plus a few niche tags in the description.
- Detected TikTok watermarks are demoted here too.
- In the app: until the Google project passes its audit, uploads come back private.

## Clipping pages

- A clipping page is judged like any page: a raw snippet is reposted content on Instagram and a likely duplicate
  for the campaign. Transform every clip (`clipping.md`, *Make it the page's own*).
- Post only on the platforms the campaign allows, from accounts linked to Whop.
