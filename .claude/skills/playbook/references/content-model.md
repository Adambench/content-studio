# Content model

Every video, whatever its format, has the same shape: a **hook** that makes a promise, **content** that builds toward
it, and a **payoff** that keeps it. A video without a named promise has nothing to wait for; a promise without a payoff
teaches the viewer (and the platform) that the hook lied. Plan the promise and the payoff before anything else, and
check both before the video renders.

## The shape

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–3 s | States the promise on three layers at once: the first spoken line, the text on screen, the first frame (`hooks.md`) |
| **Content** | the middle | Every beat moves toward the payoff: a specific, a step, a turn, proof. Re-hook between 40 % and 60 % on anything over 25 s |
| **Payoff** | the last third | Delivers exactly what the hook promised, visibly: the number said and shown, the result on screen, the answer in one line |
| **End** | right after | Stop within a few seconds. One ask at most, or a last line that loops into the first |

## The promise

- **One promise per video**, concrete enough to check: "the one setting that doubled my views", not "growth tips".
- It names what they get and hints at the cost of not knowing ("…before you post again").
- The title, caption, cover, first line and on-screen text all make the same promise.
- If the content can't keep it, change the promise, never the truth.

## Building toward it

- Open the question before you answer it. Each beat either raises the stakes, adds a specific or moves the story;
  join beats with "but" and "so", never "and then".
- Hold the payoff back. Give the second-strongest moment early (by 5–10 s) to survive the early drop, and keep the
  strongest for the payoff.
- Never close the last open question before the next one opens. A beat that answers nothing and asks nothing is cut.

## The payoff

- It lands in the second half, as late as it can while the video still feels fast: in a 30 s video around 20–27 s.
- Make it visible: a number counts up, the result is on screen, the before/after splits. Hit it with a sound effect
  on the frame it lands (never music).
- After the payoff, end within 2–8 seconds. A long tail after the payoff is where watch time leaks.

## Payoffs by format

| Format | The promise | The payoff |
|---|---|---|
| Explainer | "Why X happens" | The cause, shown in one picture or graphic |
| List | "3 things that…" | The best one, kept for last |
| Story | "This cost me $4,000" | What happened and what it taught, in one line |
| Tutorial | "Do this in 30 seconds" | The finished result on screen |
| Myth-buster | "Everyone gets X wrong" | The right way, proven |

## In the edit

Each edit names its story: the promise, the payoff and the second the payoff lands. The quality check before render
scores the hook, promise → payoff, pacing, motion, sound on motion, captions and the first frame, and a video below
the bar is fixed before it renders. Name the story whenever you build or change an edit.
