# Hooks

The hook is the first one to three seconds. Platforms show every new post to a small test group, mostly strangers,
and read whether they swipe. If the hook fails, nothing after it is seen. Spend more thought on it than on any other
part.

## Three layers, one promise

A hook runs on three layers at once, and all three point at the same idea:

| Layer | What it is | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoken line** | the first sentence said | 5 to 12 words, said in under 2 seconds. The payoff or the stake first, no warm-up. It's also indexed for search, so it names the topic |
| **On-screen text** | the hook line on screen from frame 1 | 3 to 6 words, big and bold, in the safe zone. It sharpens the spoken line, never repeats it ("I spent $400 on coffee gear" → "*$400* burned") |
| **First frame** | what the eye sees before a word lands | mid-action, the result, the conflict or a strong face. Composed as if it had just been paused, so the instinct is to play. Never a logo, a countdown, a fade-in or a face waiting to start |

Most people watch on mute: cover the sound and the text plus the frame must still stop the scroll. A sound effect
(a pop as the text lands, a whoosh on the first cut) can tie the layers together; never music.

## Match the promise

The title, caption, first frame, first line and on-screen text are one contract. The first line confirms what made
them stop, and the body delivers it. A viewer who feels tricked leaves, doesn't share, and the platform learns that
the hook overpromised. If the body can't pay a hook off, change the hook.

## Hook, retain, reward

Every short is a unit of three: the **hook** earns attention, **retain** keeps it with concrete content and proof,
the **reward** pays off with something they can use, feel or repeat. A strong hook compresses the value: the result
they want, how likely it feels, how fast and how easy. "How to [result] without [the pain they expect]" is that idea
in one line.

## Archetypes

Shapes, not sentences to fill in. Write hooks from three or four different ones and pick, instead of rewording one.

| Archetype | Shape | Use it when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contrarian** | "Everyone does X. That's why Y." | a position you can defend | biggest wins and biggest flops; the body must prove it |
| **Investigator** | "I found the [hidden thing] in [familiar place]." | revealing something they didn't know to look for | the steadiest performer, fewer flops |
| **Experimenter** | "I did X for [time] to see Y." | visible effort, tests, comparisons | the result has to be shown, not just told |
| **Teacher** | "[Who] got [result] with [unexpected method]." | advice without a lecture | name the method early |
| **Magician** | "Look at this." (the visual is the hook) | a result or effect that shows itself | the frame must be striking on its own |
| **Fortune-teller** | "This is about to change how you X." | a real shift with a demo | hype without proof reads as bait |
| **Stakes** | "Stop doing X if you want Y." / "X is costing you Y." | a cost they're paying now | the cost must be concrete |
| **Mistake** | "One [thing] cost me [number]." | a lesson with a price | name the mistake by the middle |
| **Mid-scene** | start inside the moment, no setup | stories | go straight to the turn |
| **Before/after** | "300 views. Then 40,000." | proof | say what really changed |
| **Their words** | "'I don't have time.' You have 20 minutes." | answering an objection the audience voices | use what they really say |
| **Question** | "Why does nobody talk about X?" | only when the question is sharp | weaker than a claim; never a yes/no with an obvious answer |

## Building the line

- Lead with the most interesting word. If only the first three words land, they must stop the thumb.
- Concrete: a number (odd numbers feel measured, round ones feel invented), a name, a place, a price, a time.
- One claim, said once. If the hook needs a sentence of setup, the setup was the hook. Start there.
- Strong verbs: "burned" beats "spent", "cracked" beats "figured out".
- Name the shape of the answer without giving it away: "a setting that doubles your battery" works, "you won't
  believe this" doesn't.
- Say it out loud. Over 2 seconds or a stumble: cut it.

## Score it

Score each hook 0 to 2 on five points and keep 7 or more out of 10:

| Point | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific | abstract ("grow faster") | some detail | a number, a name or a scene |
| Tension | a plain statement | mild curiosity | a gap, a stake or a contradiction that needs closing |
| Viewer | only about the speaker | the viewer is implied | the viewer sees themselves in it |
| Fast | over 15 words | 12 to 15 words | 12 words or fewer, the topic in the first 5 |
| True | the body can't pay it off | partly | the body delivers exactly that |

## Hooks for clips

A clip's hook is usually its own first line, so choosing where the clip starts *is* writing the hook (see
`clipping.md`). Start on the strongest line that works cold. When the best moment starts weakly, keep it and add a
**hook card**: on-screen text of 6 words or fewer for the first ~2.5 seconds, with one accent word, that states what
the clip is about to deliver. The hook card must be true to the clip and must never put words in the speaker's
mouth: describe the moment ("He *lost* the whole account"), don't invent a quote.

## Never open with

- a greeting, a name, "in this video", "today I'll show you", "let's talk about", "so basically"
- a logo, an intro animation, a countdown
- "Did you know" plus something everyone knows
- a vague tease ("this changed everything", "wait for it")
- "stop scrolling" or "don't swipe" (only if what follows earns that urgency)
- asking for a follow or a like before giving anything
- the brand first ("Check out our new…") instead of the viewer's result or problem
- wordplay that needs decoding: save the cleverness for the payoff
- a hook that takes its own ten seconds of setup
