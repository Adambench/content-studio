# Packaging

Packaging is everything around the video that makes someone stop, understand it and act: the first frame and cover,
the on-screen text, the title, the caption, the hashtags and the one ask. It promises what the video delivers
(`hooks.md`: one contract).

## First frame and cover

- The first frame is the thumbnail in the feed. It shows the moment, the result or the face at its most expressive,
  never a black frame, a logo or someone about to speak.
- **Instagram** shows the cover in the profile grid cropped to the centre (about 3:4 and 1:1): keep the face and the
  hook text in the middle.
- **YouTube Shorts** uses a frame from the video in the feed: the first frame should read on its own.
- Legible at thumbnail size: one subject, big text, high contrast.

## On-screen text

- **Headline + body:** a large hook line (3 to 6 words) near the top of the safe zone for the first seconds; word-
  timed captions below it carry what's said.
- **Captions always burned in.** Most people watch muted, and platform auto-captions are off by default.
- Word-by-word or short phrases in sync with the voice (the app's caption presets). Bold sans-serif, white with a
  dark outline or box, high contrast; never thin weights or pastel on busy footage.
- The body captions don't repeat the headline; they carry on from it.
- Keep everything in the safe zones (`platforms.md`).
- **Callouts:** a number, a result or a name said in a line can pop up as a short label, about one every 8 to 10
  seconds, never covering the face.

## Titles

- YouTube Shorts title: under 100 characters, ideally around 40 to 50 (only about 40 show in the feed). The topic
  word people would search for comes early.
- A clip's title names the moment, not the show: "He lost $2M in one night" beats "Podcast ep. 41 highlights".
- True to the clip: the same promise as the hook, no bigger.

## Captions

The caption is a search surface and a second hook. The video's spoken words, the on-screen text and the caption all
contain the same topic words.

| Platform | Shows before "more" | Good length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | ~80–100 characters | 50–150 characters | the keyword in the first 100 characters ranks it in search; punchy |
| Instagram | ~125 characters | ~140–150 characters, longer for story posts | captions are Instagram's main search signal now; a bit more reflective |
| YouTube Shorts | title ~40; description ~100–125 | title as above; 1 to 3 lines of description | the description's first lines and hashtags are read for search |

- **The first line is a headline:** the payoff or the tension, concrete, no setup.
- **One keyword, once, early,** plus one or two related words used naturally. No stuffing.
- **Write each platform's caption on its own.** Different cut-offs, vocabulary and tone; never paste one into all
  three.
- **Don't repeat the on-screen hook word for word.** Add context, the payoff or a new angle.
- Campaign clips: the required mentions, tags and hashtags, exactly as the brief writes them.
- No "thanks for watching", no hashtag wall, no one-emoji captions.

## Hashtags

Hashtags label the topic; they don't buy reach.

- **3 to 5 specific ones** per platform: niche tags people actually search (`#muslimentrepreneur`, not
  `#business`). One topical tag only if it really fits.
- Never `#fyp`, `#foryou`, `#viral`, `#explore`, `#trending`.
- **YouTube:** in the description, not the title; `#Shorts` plus 2 to 4 niche tags; over 15 and YouTube ignores
  them all.
- Campaign hashtags come first and don't count against the limit.

## The one ask

- **One call to action**, after the value, never before. Many asks get none.
- **Sends and saves** beat likes and follows: they're the signals that reach new people. Name the person: "Send this
  to the friend who still pays for X" works; "share with a friend" doesn't.
- A save works when there's something to come back to: a list, steps, a number to remember. Give it a name people
  can repeat ("the three-question test").
- **Engagement bait is demoted:** "like for part 2", "comment YES", "tag a friend", "follow for more", "drop a
  heart". Ask for the content of a reaction (an opinion, a story), not the act of engaging.
- A pinned comment can carry the detail people will ask for (the full list, the source, the link the brief requires).
