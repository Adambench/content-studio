# Retention

On short form, retention is distribution: platforms keep pushing what people finish, rewatch and share, and stop
pushing what they swipe away from. Structure is the mechanism, not decoration.

## Line by line

A long video is judged as a whole. A short is judged one line at a time: at every line the viewer decides whether to
stay. The only test that matters is **does this line sell the next one?** A great payoff behind a flat third line was
never seen.

## The spine

**Hook → escalation → payoff → end.**

- **Hook (0–3 s):** open a question or state a stake (`hooks.md`).
- **Escalation:** each beat raises the tension, adds a specific, or moves the story. Join beats with "but" and
  "so", not "and then". A beat joined by "and then" probably isn't earning its place.
- **Payoff:** deliver what the hook promised, clearly and visibly: show the result, say the number.
- **End:** stop. One ask at most, or a last line that loops back into the first.

## Loops

- A loop is a question the viewer is holding. **Open it before you explain**; an answer to a question nobody asked
  is just information.
- **Never close the last loop before the next one opens.** The moment they feel complete, they swipe.
- **Re-hook between 40 % and 60 %:** one line that restarts attention ("But that's not what fixed it.", "Here's the
  part everyone skips."). Under 25 seconds it can be skipped.
- The viewer has to hold the question. Someone on screen asking a question isn't a loop for the viewer unless they
  want the answer too.
- Never deflate a loop as a joke. After one fake-out, they stop believing the next.

## Value per second

- **Value promised in the first 3 seconds** sets the price of their time: make the gap feel big and the time feel
  small. Naming the cost can help ("six objections in 60 seconds").
- **Value delivered by 5–10 seconds:** the first genuinely useful or surprising thing lands early. Put the
  second-strongest moment near the top to survive the early drop, and keep the strongest for the payoff.
- **Cut the fluff:** anything that doesn't serve the hook's promise goes, however good. Preamble, "why I'm making
  this", any second or two where nothing happens, a sentence that is interesting but not relevant.
- Short sentences hold attention. Density of ideas, not of words.

## Pacing

- **Change something on screen every 2–4 seconds:** a cut, a punch-in, a reframe, a callout, a b-roll cutaway, a new
  caption page. It's not face versus screen; it's how long any one frame is held.
- **Rollercoaster, not a flat line:** fast in, a middle that breathes just enough to land one peak (the laugh, the
  reveal, the number), and a very fast end.
- **Tighten:** remove pauses, breaths, stutters, fillers and dead air, especially at the start and the end. In the
  app, `tighten_edit` does it from word timings; punch-ins on long takes come with it.
- **Two channels:** what's said and what's shown can tell complementary things (the voice explains, the b-roll
  shows). Never contradictory.
- Fast editing can't save a broken loop, and a strong chain of loops can carry a long shot.

## Endings

- **End abruptly**, right after the payoff. No "thanks for watching", no summary, no outro: they signal "over" and
  people leave before the loop.
- **Loop it:** the last line or frame leads back into the first, so the replay feels continuous. Under 15 seconds,
  a loop is the way to get past 100 % watched.
- One call to action at most, after the value, never before it (`packaging.md`).

## Length follows retention

Pick the length the payoff needs, never pad. Directional benchmarks creators report (not published by the
platforms):

| Length | Healthy average watched | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| under 15 s | over 100 % | it has to loop |
| 15–30 s | ~65–70 % | one sharp point |
| 30–60 s | ~50 % | at least one re-hook |
| over 60 s | ~40 % | two re-hooks, stacked payoffs |

21–34 s clips tend to finish best; most clips land between 20 and 60 s.

## Diagnosing a flop

Three failure modes, read from the retention curve or from the draft:

1. **Gone by second 1–3 (cliff at the start):** the hook. Setup first, slow first line, a logo, a promise the frame
   doesn't match. Rewrite the first three seconds, nothing else.
2. **Bleeds out (steady fall):** no escalation. Add a loop early, reorder so tension rises, cut beats that don't
   raise the stakes, add a re-hook.
3. **Stalls before the end (late cliff):** the payoff is buried. Move it (or a taste of it) earlier, shorten the
   setup, tease it in the hook.

When several posts in a row underperform, it's almost always the hook or the format, not the topic. Read it once as
a viewer, mark the exact second attention would drop, and fix that beat.

## Objections

Every video creates its own reason to swipe: "this is an ad", "this needs skills I don't have", "this only works
because they're big", "I've seen this", "this won't work for me". Know the one this video provokes and disarm it in
a line, right where the doubt lands.
